## Classification
A popular science book. Specifically, it looks at a new theory in physics that tries to answer the question of how life evolves. How do these pieces fit together in the universe? How did something come from nothing? How did we make the switch from physics to biology?

## The Book in a Sentence
An intro to assembly theory and an explanation of how life begins.

## The Problem
How does the universe get from physics to biology?

She succeeded in showing what assembly theory would predict the answer to be. With popular science books, especially theory explanation books, it's a theory. We don't know if it's right. I don't know if the theory is right, but she gave a good explanation of what it is and helped me add it as a framework to my mental models. She definitely succeeded on that account.

## Key Terms
Pulled from the highlights.

- **Assembly theory.** Things combine to get to the next stepping stone, with many different combinations up the stack. It's Lego blocks all the way down. Her statement of it: observing a complex object implies that the information about the steps of its formation exists in other objects, "a physically instantiated memory," which can happen only via evolution or learning.
- **Assembly index / minimal path.** The shortest path to build an object. It is intrinsic to the object: ATP has the same assembly index at the bottom of an ocean on Earth or on Enceladus.
- **Selection before life.** Selection among the space of what is possible happens before life. "The physics of what gets to exist."
- **Constructor / memory.** The information that must be remembered for an object to be generated. The LEGO object cannot exist without something like you to build it.
- **Causation vs. control.** Dennett's boulder and skier. All things are caused, but not all things that are caused are controlled. Control requires an agent.
- **Emergence and complexity.** "Life is the vaporware of chemistry." The coffee and cream swirl is a good definition of complexity.

## Key Ideas
- Life is a marker of how many combinations there are. Before a certain point, things combine naturally by hitting into each other. Life is the continual combinations after that point, self-sustaining beyond the random combinations of various parts.
- She did a really good job of understanding and explaining what assembly theory actually does. She brought in arguments from different areas, walked through the logic, and helped me understand what assembly theory even is and what it would mean for it to be true.
- First contact with alien life may come from evolving it from scratch in the lab, not from finding it on another planet. This would actually be awesome. If we can generate many types of life on Earth, maybe we can inoculate ourselves against the risk of a catastrophic impact with new bases of life later on. (My note at the time said this might mitigate "my earlier worry." I no longer remember the worry. Best guess: that interacting with other life would lead to disease taking one side or the other.)
- Boltzmann brains are impossible under assembly theory, not just improbable. I wonder if this would be testable.

## Disagreements
I don't think she spent much time on why assembly theory might be wrong, or on what evidence there is against it.

## Takeaways
It didn't change what I do. But it adds a way of thinking about how complexity grows. Lego blocks all the way down is a decent mental model.
