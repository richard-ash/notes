# Xi Jinping's Ideology and Grand Strategy

Kevin Rudd's account of Xi Jinping — from a July 2026 Ezra Klein Show interview built on Rudd's Oxford doctorate (published as *On Xi Jinping: How Xi's Marxist Nationalism Is Shaping China and the World*), for which he read everything published under Xi's name. Rudd's vantage is unusually multi-angled: Australian foreign-service China analyst in 1980s Beijing (he first met Xi in the mid-80s as vice mayor of Xiamen), prime minister of Australia (hours-long fireside conversations in Mandarin with then-vice-president Xi), Australian ambassador to the US, now president of the Asia Society. His refrain to American interlocutors: **never underestimate Xi Jinping.**

Confidence is `medium` because this is one (expert, but opinionated) analyst's synthesis, and Rudd repeatedly flags where he is hypothesizing — especially on elite politics ("we weren't in the room") and PLA purge motives.

## The core worldview: unity at home, adversaries divided abroad

Rudd's fireside takeaway from Xi before he became paramount leader: Xi reads two thousand years of Chinese history as showing China is a great power when it is (a) unified and (b) able to keep foreign adversaries under control and divided — and collapses when neither holds. The Chinese Communist Party's contemporary mandate follows: maintain domestic unity, keep China free from external pressure, and return it to global-great-power status. Xi never said "I have a unique mandate to do this," but Rudd found the subtext unmistakable — a man who located himself inside unfolding Chinese history. This unity-as-precondition reading rhymes with the dynastic collapse cycle in [[medieval-chinese-warfare]] and inverts the "efficient market of civilizations" claim in [[geography-shapes-history]] that unity produces stagnation.

## Formation: the purge that produced loyalty, not hatred

Xi's father, Xi Zhongxun, was a trusted Mao lieutenant from the Yan'an years who was repeatedly purged, including through the Cultural Revolution. Rudd argues purges in Leninist systems produce one of two psychologies: hostility to the party as an abuse of power, or *deeper* loyalty ("you have recognized your errors through criticism and self-criticism"). Both father and son landed in the second category — Rudd invokes Orwell's Winston loving Big Brother. Americans assume the natural reaction is the first (Klein notes people purged from Trump's GOP going down "alternative paths"); Rudd says Leninist induction instead forges "veins of steel": unemotional, hard-headed, absolutely resolute that the party is the engine carrying China to great-power status.

## The ascent: princeling + safe hands + masked ambition

- **Hong'erdai (second-generation red):** being Xi Zhongxun's son was a career asset once the father was rehabilitated. Xi's chief rival, Bo Xilai, was also a princeling; Xi's circle "dealt with" him.
- **Technocratic record:** private secretary to defense minister Geng Biao (his claim to military grounding), then prefecture party secretary, vice mayor of Xiamen SEZ, party secretary of Fujian, Zhejiang, and Shanghai — provinces at the reformist cutting edge, with growth numbers proving he wasn't "a political show pony."
- **The kingmaker's bet:** Jiang Zemin, worried about industrial-scale corruption and post-1989 discipline slide, picked Xi as a party-first man who would nonetheless keep the reform program safe. Rudd's judgment: Jiang would have been "deeply surprised" by how far Xi swung the political economy leftward from ~2015 — more central planning, less space for entrepreneurs, and the rehabilitated Maoist formula that "the party leads in all" (government, military, academy, commerce, society).
- Rudd frames the whole climb via Machiavelli: mask your ambitions while serving the prince.

## The ideology: Marxist-Leninist nationalism

Rudd's synthesis from the corpus — Xi is a **convinced Marxist dialectician**, and the epistemology matters more than any single doctrine:

- **Dialectical materialism:** history advances through struggle between progressive and reactionary forces (working class vs. capitalist class) toward a communist synthesis. Xi has rehabilitated "struggle" as a live term after it largely disappeared under Deng, Jiang, and Hu.
- **Historical materialism:** the parallel discipline asking *where are we now* in that inexorable process. The party's answer: China is exiting the "primary stage of socialism" for something higher, and the party under Xi should accelerate the change. Applied to nation-states, Xi's favorite phrases — "changes not seen in a century" (first used in 2017, on the Bolshevik revolution's centenary) and "the rise of the East and the decline of the West" — are euphemisms for the claim that China's time has come.
- **The nationalist right shift:** simultaneous with the Marxist-left turn, a growing appeal to civilizational achievement and the "hundred years of humiliation" (Opium Wars → 1945). The nation becomes the vessel through which the Marxist-Leninist project is realized and exported.
- Klein's caveat, which Rudd endorses: Westerners find the *teleology* — history having a direction you can accelerate — the hardest part to believe anyone believes. Rudd insists it would be "analytically derelict" not to take it seriously as the actual analytical mechanism of the CCP's central organs.

A methodological aside worth keeping: Klein and Rudd agree that **what politicians say in public is a steadier signal than what they say in private** — public words set parameters leaders must live within; private comments shade to the audience. (Rudd on Xi: "words are bullets for these guys.")

## The Soviet collapse as training video

Xi's reading of 1991 — the negative image of the account in [[why-russia-lost-the-cold-war]] — has three lessons, per Rudd:

1. **Glasnost/perestroika ≈ reform-and-opening gone unguarded:** unleash capitalist impulses too far and you shake the party's disciplinary structures.
2. **Ideological corrosion precedes physical corruption** ("the fish rots from the head") — hence his 2013 speech to the propaganda apparatus on the attrition of communist values in the late USSR.
3. **Not one man in the Soviet Red Army stood up to defend the party in 1991** — hence absolute party control of the PLA.

Rudd also dates the deeper fork earlier: Tiananmen (he was in the square in May–June 1989) was the party deciding not to be the Soviet Union, and after Deng died in 1997 the internal debate about a Singapore-style guided-democracy "exit ramp" concluded with "no — we're hanging on for the long term," a decade before Xi took power.

## The control machinery

- **Anti-corruption as control:** launched immediately on taking office (end of 2012). Rudd's judgment: control is "the dominant factor" — the organization department holds a file on everyone, some of it fabricated; power lies in choosing whose file to pull.
- **Civil society:** from 2013, directives reining in religion (unauthorized practice, not all practice), journalists, rights lawyers, and village-level democracy experiments — each read as erosion of Leninist control. China in 2026 is "vastly less free" than a decade prior.
- **Military purges:** ~100 senior PLA generals in recent years, including CMC vice chairman Zhang Youxia, a lifelong Xi family friend. Rudd's two-part reading: genuine belief that corruption impedes Taiwan-mission capability, plus intolerance of internal dissent — his (flagged) hypothesis being that officers voicing doubts about Taiwan readiness are read as "sources of doubt" in a system meant to project absolute confidence. He rejects the American inference that purges make action against Taiwan less likely: "for everyone purged, there's a queue of 100 people waiting to take the job."
- **Surveillance:** the blank-paper ("white card") protesters were tracked via phone location and payments and "whisked away." Iris, gait, and facial recognition plus all-electronic payments give the state tools "Mao and Stalin would have dreamed of."

## The end of the growth guarantee

The old unofficial social contract — party legitimacy bought with ~8% growth (then 6%, then "four would be okay") — has been deliberately traded away. Xi's response to sliding growth runs at three levels: double down on ideology (produce "little pinks"), lean on the surveillance state, and re-engineer the economy around the **supply side** — manufacturing, industry, high technology, and control of supply chains at home and progressively worldwide — explicitly so China cannot be pressured externally in a future Taiwan crisis. The cost is suppressed private consumption (high savings rate, low consumption for a middle-income country), youth unemployment, and disenchantment ("lying flat"); the prescribed answer is *chi ku*, "eat bitterness," backed by the surveillance apparatus.

**The platform crackdown and partial correction:** Jack Ma's Bund Conference attack on party financial regulation triggered the crackdown on Ant, Tencent, and the platform companies, then a broader campaign against ostentatious private wealth, implanted party secretaries, and state equity stakes. Consumption *and* private fixed investment slid, so from ~2024 the party stabilized: a floor under the collapsed property sector, Ma brought in from the cold in early 2025 — partly because Xi realized AI-led "new productive forces" live mostly in the private sector, which state enterprises cannot substitute for. Rudd's emphasis: these are corrections *at the margins*; the Marxist-left, industrial-policy, supply-chain-control orientation is unchanged.

## The industrial-subsidy strategy — and its clock

Rudd's account of the mechanism behind the "industrial juggernaut" (Made in China 2025 as the largest industrial-policy exercise in economic history; the ten 2015 technology targets — semiconductors, aeronautics, EVs, batteries — largely reached by 2025):

- The subsidy is **temporary predation by design**: subsidize to a scale that destroys the world's alternative producers, let them fold, then restore prices once alternative sources of production are gone.
- It is financed by "borrowing from Chinese consumers and their personal savings in banks" — suppressed domestic prosperity converted into industrial scale — and **cannot be sustained indefinitely**.
- With the US tariff wall up, surplus production "all roads lead to Europe," driving the European political reaction.
- The rare-earths case study: dominance so complete China could break Trump's trade war by threatening access to rare earths and magnets. "They had leverage over us that we did not have over them" — an engineer-dominated political culture that instinctively maps the periodic table of leverage.
- Rudd's warning about American perception whiplash (China-is-breaking circa 2023 → China-is-ahead circa 2026): "hope becomes a substitute for analysis and strategy." Whatever the inefficiency, Xi regards it as loose change against the commanding heights.

## Xi's view of America

Two lenses: America is the only country with the **scale** (military, alliances, innovation) to stand in the way, and it has represented for a century an **alternative ideational script** — a live attraction, since successful Chinese still emigrate toward freedom. The party's "comprehensive national power" framework aggregates military size, alliance solidity, and domestic political unity/disunity; it stopped publishing results ~12 years ago, Rudd suspects because they showed China rising too clearly. The mainstream CCP view holds America in structural decline ("rise of the East, decline of the West").

On US policy: the sea change was the 2017 National Security Strategy's shift from **strategic engagement to strategic competition** ("you could almost hear the creaking of the entire machinery of the US state"), sustained through Biden. At the recent Beijing summit the *Chinese* proposed a new organizing principle — **"constructive strategic stability"** — which the White House accepted "so long as it's fair and reciprocal." Rudd decodes it as an attempt to make the US more stable, less unpredictable, less antagonistic; its practical content is unclear beyond nascent US–China negotiation on regulating dangerous AI. This is the same order-contest [[liberal-international-order]] describes from the American side.

## Rudd's prescription: managed strategic competition

Against both hawkish closure and naive openness, three simultaneous tracks:

1. **Red lines + deterrence** — Taiwan, South China Sea, East China Sea; managing these creates the stability for everything else.
2. **Open competition everywhere else** — trade, capital, technology, talent, ideas, and the shape of the international order: "may the best system win."
3. **Defined cooperation lanes** — AI governance, pandemic prevention, global public health.

"We can walk and chew gum in these three domains while recognizing we're not organizing a Sunday school picnic."

## Taiwan: the 2028 miscalculation window

- Xi wants unification before the PRC centenary in **2049** — "now 23 years away." His language has hardened (warning Trump in Beijing of "collision and conflict" if mishandled — formulations he had not used before), and PLA exercises have moved from abstract preparedness to **rehearsals** for seizing an offshore island, blockade, or full invasion, including replica Taiwanese buildings visible on satellite imagery.
- Rudd's specific worry is **2028**: Taiwan's January 2028 election could return the independence-leaning DPP for a fourth term (he puts it at 50/50) just after Xi's expected reappointment, in a US presidential election year, with American appetite for intervention depleted and arsenal depth degraded by the Iran war. The danger is not a calculated plan but **miscalculation through excessive self-confidence** about American resolve — colliding with a US president who above all hates looking weak.
- Why Americans should care, in Rudd's hard-headed ordering: (1) the Taiwan Relations Act (1979) makes Congress custodian of a de facto security guarantee; (2) a fallen Taiwan ends Pax Americana in allied consciousness — Japan plausibly goes nuclear, then Korea, and the region's dynamic transforms (the maritime-order credibility logic of [[continental-vs-maritime-powers]]); (3) no peace dividend follows — Xi has made plain that power projection is pan-regional and increasingly global; (4) the values question: indifference to 23 million people in an organically evolved democracy reads as indifference to freedom worldwide.
- The wise policy is also the cheapest: **sustain deterrence and the status quo**, and per Deng, kick the ball down the road to wiser generations.
