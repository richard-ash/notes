# History

Geopolitics, grand strategy, and historical analysis.

## Articles
- [[continental-vs-maritime-powers]] — Paine's foundational framework: continental empires (Russia, China) pursue security through territorial conquest; maritime powers (Britain, US) pursue prosperity through trade. The three discriminating factors (sea defense / threat geometry / lines of communication), Britain's six-rule elephant-hunting playbook, the industrial revolution as regime change, and sanctions as economic chemotherapy
- [[why-russia-lost-the-cold-war]] — Prof. Paine's multi-causal analysis of the Soviet collapse: arms race pressure, Helsinki/human rights, economic rot, Gorbachev's miscalculations, and Bush-Kohl diplomacy
- [[liberal-international-order]] — Zakaria's argument that the post-1945 U.S.-built order is giving way to a "predatory hegemon," with the Trump-era Iran war as turning point; British Empire overextension parallel
- [[mexico-political-history]] — Gillingham's 500-year reading of Mexico: hands-off federalism as survival strategy, the Revolution's qualitative redistribution, regional patterns of leadership and violence, and why judicial elections are a disaster
- [[inventing-the-renaissance]] — Ada Palmer on the Renaissance as a 200-year Roman-cosplay project that failed on its own terms and accidentally produced the scientific revolution; Florence's weird merchant republic; the printing press as a 150-year cascade; the Inquisition accidentally inventing peer review; why censors are always wrong
- [[medieval-chinese-warfare]] — Graff's survey of Chinese warfare 300-900 AD via Psmith: the dynastic collapse cycle, "Yellow" vs "Blue" China, Turkic-Chinese synthesis producing the Tang, and Yu Gun's ordered community as the real antidote to civilizational collapse
- [[geography-shapes-history]] — Pueyo's "chessboard" thesis: geography sets History's probabilities, not its outcomes. The US as "inevitable empire" (Mississippi basin + intracoastal waterways = more navigable water than the rest of the world combined, plus free defense), Diamond's east-west vs. north-south axes explaining why Eurasia out-developed Africa and the Americas, and the "efficient market of civilizations" claim that competition-without-unity drives progress (Tokugawa Japan and Ming China as stagnation-through-unity counter-cases)
- [[ptolemaic-egypt]] — Toby Wilkinson on the Greek-ruled commercial Egypt of Alexander to Cleopatra (332–30 BC): Alexandria as the "Manhattan of the ancient world," the Library/science cluster and why it formed there (mummification → anatomy; patronage; book-thieving), gold-and-grain economy with franchised monopolies and a two-tier ethnic labor market, why moderate ambition kept it from becoming Rome, the fiscal-overstretch-plus-climate decline, and Cleopatra as genuinely consequential
- [[xi-jinping-ideology]] — Kevin Rudd's synthesis of Xi's Marxist-Leninist nationalism: unity-at-home/adversaries-divided worldview, purge-forged party loyalty, the Soviet collapse as training video, anti-corruption as control, the end of the 8%-growth social contract in favor of a temporarily-subsidized supply-side juggernaut, "constructive strategic stability," managed strategic competition, and the 2028 Taiwan miscalculation window
- [[military-technology-and-political-centralization]] — Fitzsimmons's thesis that weapons *access*, governed by supply-chain geography rather than weapon quality, sets the ceiling on political centralization: bronze's thousand-mile tin trade made empires, locally smeltable (and metallurgically worse) iron kept them from reconsolidating after 1200 BC, with skeletal-trauma, battle-database, and king-list evidence; offense-defense vs. access as the two axes; drones as today's iron and AI as ambiguous between tin and bog iron
- [[war-termination]] — Paine's closing lecture: wars are easy to start, hard to end, hardest to end on your terms. Clausewitz's limited/unlimited objectives, culminating points of attack and victory (third-party intervention as the overshoot alarm), decisive vs. pivotal vs. lopsided battles, the Bologna flask, Sun Tzu's death ground, value of the object. Russo-Japanese War as model termination (exit strategy via Roosevelt chosen before the first shot; five candidate culminating points), Gulf War I as the isolated-enemy ideal, Korea as overshooting twice, then the two pathologies: regional wars going global (1778, 1914, 1941) and insurgency as victory denial (Washington's design, Japan in China, Iraq 2003). Ends with a pre-war checklist
- [[victorian-ruling-class]] — Hughes's case that Britain's nineteenth-century elite ran the largest empire in history on soft skills: boarding schools where classics were a MacGuffin for character, 10–20 hours a week of team sport, prefect self-government as "elite liberalism," Oxbridge pass degrees and sherry tutorials, then a five-month London Season of 1am balls that doubled as the political information network. A Tönnies *Gemeinschaft* at the apex of a *Gesellschaft*, whose closure let prestige substitute for pay (unpaid MPs spending £150k–450k per election) and police defection (Bagehot's patriotic statesmen); read against Wiener's anti-industrial-culture decline thesis and the 1854–1911 meritocratic reforms Hughes leaves out
