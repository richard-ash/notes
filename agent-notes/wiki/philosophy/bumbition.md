# Bumbition

**Bumbition** ("bum" + "ambition") is Noah Smith's name for a life path that sits off the usual striver-versus-slacker line: years without a definite goal, spent drifting, playing and exploring, followed by fast recognition of one compelling opportunity and years of very hard work on it. Smith coined the term in November 2026 and offers it as an overlooked route to success, not a better one.

## The claim

Smith's central move is that bumbition is **not intermittent ambition**. The two run on different motives, so they succeed for different reasons:

- **The ambitious person** always has a goal ("make partner by 30", "start a unicorn", "conquer Central Asia"), whether self-set or handed down, and the payoff is the *feeling of personal success*. A brass ring is always available, so the ambitious person is never idle.
- **The bumbitious person** is moved by the wish to do something *intrinsically interesting or worthwhile*. Such projects are scarce and the test for them is subjective, so the bumbitious person may wait years or decades with no goal at all.

He grants that neither path is better on average; the greats of every field include both kinds. His complaint is that culture treats only one of them as legitimate, telling every unemployed gamer to get back to the grind (and he notes that China treats its "lying flat" generation as a national-security threat).

## The case file

Smith's anchor cases are two founders of TypeSafe AI, maker of Jev, a model that skips human language to decide faster and cheaper than text-based LLMs, valued at $7.5 billion in October 2026:

- **Sasha Sheng**, a mid-level Facebook engineer who read ML papers as a hobby, quit in the pandemic, and spent years making clothes, learning ballet, posting Instagram videos and winning hackathons.
- **Diogo Almeida**, an International Math Olympiad performer and data scientist who spent his gap years on a girlfriend's couch becoming a top-ranked Hearthstone player, learning to skateboard and going on retreats. A footnote adds that he had already been a key OpenAI researcher on the original ChatGPT and GPT-4.

Smith discloses a small investment in TypeSafe. The historical cases are Steve Jobs (communes, India, calligraphy), Jack Dorsey, Larry Ellison and Mitch Kapor; Ulysses S. Grant (drinking, a leather-store clerkship and failed ventures before the Civil War); Charles Darwin (hunting and riding instead of studying medicine); James Cameron (a truck driver until *Star Wars*); Kevin Smith (a clerk until *Slacker*); and Haruki Murakami (a jazz-bar owner who decided one day to write).

## Costs

Smith names two:

1. **The call may not come, or may come unseen.** Waiting is a high-variance bet. The opportunity can arrive late in life, or arrive and go unrecognized.
2. **Waiting decays the waiter.** Technical skill and work ethic erode, and an unstructured life leaves more room for bad relationships, addiction and depression.

He closes on the same caution: telling yourself you are "waiting for the true call" does not mean you will hear it. Bumbition requires actually wanting to do something great and being ready to work flat out for years when it shows up.

## Benefits

**Ambition keeps people busy.** Someone who must always be working toward a goal, and can't find a great one, will take a mediocre one. Smith's counterfactuals: a perfect GPA at Reed might have crowded out Apple; a successful leather store might have kept Grant out of the Army. He borrows Peter Thiel's account in *Zero to One* of narrowly losing a Supreme Court clerkship, which Thiel calls a lucky loss because winning would have locked him into "taking depositions or drafting other people's business deals." He also quotes Paul Graham's split between ambition that *precedes* interest in a subject and ambition that *grows out of* it, and Graham's line that planning "only works for achievements you can describe in advance … you can't discover natural selection that way."

**The bum phase is not a holding pattern.** Smith argues it does two jobs, *waiting and exploration* and *personal development*, through three channels:

1. **Perspective.** Original ideas often come from recombining fields, and a change of surroundings can help creativity. His examples: Feynman, burned out, deciding to "play" with physics, working out a wobbling cafeteria plate and following it to quantum electrodynamics; Jobs's calligraphy class shaping the Mac's typography; a friend who carried a camera technique from photography into a new neuroimaging method.
2. **Interests.** Following Graham's "excited curiosity", the bum phase is time to *play around* until something catches. Constant grinding leaves little room to grow curiosity.
3. **Networks.** Strivers meet people inside their own field. The unemployed meet everyone, which is how they hear about odd opportunities, borrow ideas from other fields and find co-founders.

## The AI argument

Smith ends on the claim that AI raises the stakes. Models are "consummate hill-climbers", more relentless than any Stanford law student once given a task and data. If execution gets cheap, the scarce human job becomes *choosing which hill*, and that takes exploration, unusual perspective and curiosity, the things a bum phase builds. So, he concludes, we should teach young people to be more bumbitious. This is the same shape as the [[prediction-machines]] argument that cheap prediction raises the value of its complement, judgment; Smith's version moves the complement from judging outcomes to picking goals.

## Reading it against the rest of the wiki

**Graham supplies the theory, Smith the life-scale version.** [[doing-great-work]] already argues against planning creative work, recommends "staying upwind" by doing whatever seems most interesting, and warns that per-project procrastination hides inside productive-looking work on the wrong thing. Smith's "ambition keeps people overly busy" is that warning seen from outside: from a distance the busy, wrong-project person looks ambitious, not like a procrastinator. Graham's [[organic-startup-ideation]] ("notice" ideas, don't "think them up") is the same mechanism inside a single startup search.

**Compounding is the strongest objection.** [[you-and-your-research|Hamming]]'s "intellectual compound interest" (an extra hour a day, sustained, more than doubles a career's output) and the learning loop in [[superlinear-returns]] both say that years off the curve forfeit growth that never comes back. Smith concedes skill decay but doesn't weigh it against compounding. His own cases suggest a reconciliation he leaves implicit: the play that paid off was *next to* a deep skill already banked. Sheng read ML papers for fun while employed as an engineer; Almeida had the IMO and OpenAI behind him; Feynman played with physics, not with something unrelated. The bum phase looks less like a substitute for compounding than a sabbatical taken after a large deposit, spent re-aiming the capital rather than adding to it. Hamming's open-door finding (less output per day, but better problems) is a milder, in-job version of Smith's network channel.

**Selection on the outcome.** Every case was picked because the call came. The base rate of talented people who bummed and never heard one is invisible, the survivorship pattern [[probability-neglect]] describes. The cases also share a buffer: a Facebook salary, an OpenAI résumé, a girlfriend's couch. That suggests bumbition is cheapest for people who already have savings and credentials, which matters for Smith's prescription to "teach" it broadly. None of this refutes Smith, who claims parity, not superiority, but it does limit what the case file can show.

**Gardner and Thiel.** [[self-renewal|Gardner]]'s barnacle, which picks one rock and cements its head there for life, is the ambitious failure mode Smith describes, and Gardner's split between ambition (which wears out) and zest (which lasts) tracks Smith's split between success-seeking and interest-seeking. Thiel is an awkward witness. Smith uses Thiel's clerkship story against chasing standard prizes, yet Thiel's [[definite-optimism]] scorns the open-options, see-what-happens posture that a bum phase resembles from outside. One way to square them is that bumbition is indefinite while waiting and definite once called; Smith's insistence on years of hard work after the call is what keeps it from being plain indefinite optimism.

**Agency is the switch.** The moment that separates a bumbitious person from a lifelong bum is recognizing the opportunity and acting at once, which is close to Cate Hall's [[high-agency|"manifest determination to make things happen"]]. Smith's framework says little about how that switch is built; Hall's claim that agency is learnable is one candidate answer.

**Smith's own earlier argument.** In [[consumption-identity]] Smith argued that identity can come from what we consume as much as what we produce, and that AI may loosen the tie between identity and work. Bumbition is the work-side sequel: the consumption years (ballet, Hearthstone, calligraphy) are the raw material the later production is made from.
