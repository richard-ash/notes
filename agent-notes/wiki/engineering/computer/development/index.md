- [[technical-clarity]] — Goedecke's account of the staff engineer's highest-leverage job: giving non-technical decision-makers a good-enough working model of the system. - [[understanding-as-the-bottleneck]] — Herrengt's rate-asymmetry thesis: AI made *producing* changes cheap while *understanding* and *repairing* them stayed slow, so bad decisions now compound faster than they can be drained ("by the time you've untangled one bad decision, five more have been merged") and AI "makes projects with weak engineering culture fail much faster"; the process controls don't hold because the difficulty of producing code *was* one of the limits, leaving a trilemma (generate less / validate better / accept lower quality); design-decision provenance evaporates into unsummarizable chat logs so the org loses not universal comprehension but the *someone* who understood; the compiler objection answered by determinism-and-semantics-preservation vs. an LLM making dozens of design decisions, with an explicit falsification condition (reliable spec-to-code verification); the productivity-transfer argument where 10 PRs/day relocates work onto the scarcest attention rather than creating 10×; the salary-bifurcation *bet* (implementation is cheap, the premium was never for typing) flagged as under-evidenced against [[decide-execute-deliver-sandwich]]; and the junior-developer inversion where juniors using AI to build understanding beat seniors who stopped trying
- [[system-design]] — Goedecke's practitioner survey of assembling *services* (not lines of code): good design looks underwhelming, statefulness is the axis everything else hangs off (one service owns the state; caching, long-horizon queues and idempotency keys are all corollaries), database bottlenecks and read-replica routing, background jobs as the default for slow work, events only when the producer doesn't care or volume is latency-insensitive, push-vs-pull, hot paths as the design-attention budget, unhappy-path logging and p95/p99 over averages, and killswitches/circuit-breakers/fail-open-vs-fail-closed
