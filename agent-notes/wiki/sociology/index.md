# Sociology

Cultural evolution, social structure, normative order, demographic dynamics, and the mechanisms by which group behaviors propagate, drift, and select.

## Articles
- [[unicontext]] — Agnes Callard's thesis that one set of norms now applies in every situation, replacing the many local normative worlds humans historically inhabited: the normative-vs-informational refinement of context collapse (the peasant village was informationally collapsed but normatively plural), technology as dialectical partner to a human aversion to "world closure" rather than sole cause, and four derived consequences — online negativity (badness is universal, goodness is context-dependent, so bads are the reliable coordination point), identity displacing character (identity is the hat you never take off; character requires many contexts to perceive), comparison-then-homogenization via shared evaluative fields (school choice, Moneyball), and top-down attention management (no 19th-century novel heroine ever told herself to get through another twenty pages) — plus the 1910 modernist inflection where fragmentation is the early phase of the same process whose late phase is sameness, Simmel's money-as-abstraction as the deeper thread, and a deliberately withheld verdict on whether it's good or bad since "the unicontext can only see bads"
- [[cultural-drift-maladaptation]] — Hanson's argument that humanity has broken its cultural-evolution-by-natural-selection engine: collapse of cultural variety into a monoculture, near-elimination of selection pressure (war/famine/disease), and acceleration of environmental change and activist-driven drift have moved key parameters into a region where cultural species-defining features should drift into maladaptation; differential fertility is the only remaining selection mechanism, leaving insular fertile subcultures (Amish, Haredim) as the projected demographic inheritors
