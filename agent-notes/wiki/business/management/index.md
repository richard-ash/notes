# Business > Management

Management theory, leadership philosophy, and organizational practice.

## Articles

- [[infra-product-management]] — transitioning infrastructure teams from maintenance to innovation using product management: discovery, prioritization, and solution validation
- [[its-not-magic]] — John Amaechi (ex-NBA, Exeter prof) on leadership as a set of earned, chosen behaviors — rituals, genuine care, "culture is the worst behavior tolerated," and why personality testing is bollocks
- [[scaling-people]] — Psmith's critical review of Claire Hughes Johnson's *Scaling People*, arguing management requires domain expertise (not "management turpentine") and that leadership requires genuine care for subordinates
- [[deliberate-want]] — Rands on treating reqs as perishable and consistently telling candidates "we want you" throughout the hiring process to prevent last-minute walkbacks
- [[hiring-for-curiosity]] — Julian Shapiro on indexing for curiosity over mission-passion or experience, customizing roles to a candidate's learning agenda, and the two interview questions that surface real curious behavior
- [[technical-leverage]] — investing in technology to multiply the business value a development group delivers; embed innovation in product work rather than separating it into a dedicated team
- [[how-to-operate]] — Keith Rabois's framework for startup leadership: CEO as editor, barrels vs ammunition, delegation via conviction-consequence matrix, radical focus, pairing metrics, and obsessing over details
- [[manager-anti-patterns]] — Will Larson's taxonomy of ways engineering managers get stuck, organized by experience level: directional imbalances, identity traps, delegation failures, and attention management
- [[product-management-framework]] — Larson's three-phase PM framework for engineering leaders: problem discovery (seven lenses), problem selection (time-horizon stress testing), and solution validation (cheap experimentation over analysis)
- [[ic-anti-patterns]] — Fournier's taxonomy of how ICs get stuck: eight sidetrack patterns (analysis paralysis, infinite research, compulsive refactoring) and twelve stuck patterns (the last 10–20%, starting from scratch, asking for help)
- [[first-team-mindset]] — Wong on leaders prioritizing their peer leadership group over direct reports: explicit expectations, cohort-building, normalized interdependence, and inviting vulnerability
- [[truth-telling-leadership]] — Horowitz on the trust–communication inverse relation, why honesty feels suicidal but lying is fatal, and how leaders should pair hard facts with assigned meaning (Gettysburg as case study)
- [[tech-influencer]] — Larson on content creation as career strategy: a few well-distributed pieces beat prolific output, prestige is the real lever, and most influence metrics are bad proxies
- [[hiring-market-for-lemons]] — Dan Luu's critique of Spolsky's "great devs are sticky" thesis: the model is internally inconsistent, information asymmetry often favors prospective employers, and hiring struggles reduce to four fixable problems (low pay, bad filters, rare skill combos, hidden dysfunction)
- [[ai-and-org-design]] — Taylor's "atomic unit of AI productivity is a process, not a person" thesis: companies ship their org charts, supplier onboarding crosses four departments and no one owns it, why Copilot rollouts feel incremental, the hyper-generalist with an AI exoskeleton, and flatter post-AI org structures
- [[dream-job-framework]] — Gurley's five principles for thriving in a career you love: find your passion, hone your craft obsessively, develop mentors, embrace peers, and pay it forward; illustrated through Bobby Knight, Bob Dylan, Danny Meyer, Sam Hinkie, and Katrina Lake
- [[elon-operating-philosophy]] — Jorgenson's *Book of Elon* synthesis: the multiplicative thesis (not 10,000× smarter, 10,000× more effective via stacked methods that multiply), the named Algorithm (question requirements → delete → optimize → accelerate → automate, in that order; reverse order is the typical pathology), maniacal urgency priced in lost future revenue, demos beat decks, mission as recruiting substrate, empathy for mission over individual, vector-sum teams with one metric per era, thinking-in-limits, S-curve stacking with vision-expansion when metrics become measurable, and the "20 Elon clones" pattern (Mr. Beast-style algorithm transfer beats domain expertise — Starlink turnaround)
- [[ai-era-engineering-leadership]] — Larson's five revised engineering-leadership rules after a year of AI tooling at Imprint: migrations done by individuals (raising the premium on judgment), first-pass code free but working code gated by your dev harness, optimize the base case of every process for agents, durable domain-context teams matter more not less, and fast binding decision-making as the prerequisite to benefit from AI — each tied to named projects (6→200-400 deploys/week, 0→100% AI adoption, multi-year migrations compressed to a quarter)
- [[executive-role]] — Pennarun (apenwarr) on Grove's *High Output Management*: the executive is the least-informed person in the room, so their job is to ratify decisions made by those closest to the facts (the lowest-common-boss meeting as forcing function) and enforce values — not to decide, break ties, or set strategy; four failure modes, values-produce-strategy, values-as-tradeoffs, and why strategy change means executive replacement
- [[ai-mania]] — Ludic's field report on AI hype as an organizational-behavior pathology: five self-reinforcing mechanisms (unmeasurable-by-design investment failure, heretics-get-shot selection against honesty, the demo-as-mind-killer buying frenzy, the executive prisoner's-dilemma/"government by assassination," and the AI purity test that relabels ordinary projects), plus a survival playbook (anonymous 1–10 success polls, one-on-one dissent, don't challenge the broad claim, and job-search cues)
- [[founder-mode-investment-firms]] — Kapadia's argument that founder mode applies to investing organizations: "keep the rope tight" anti-decay discipline, David Chang's "good enough plus something special" hiring rubric applied equally to investment and non-investment seats, open-plan office as a connectivity bet for cross-domain (software/power/semis) theses, and founder mode as the prerequisite for AI adoption in SEC-regulated firms — plus skepticism of headline-driven AI spend (the J.P. Morgan $2B announcement)
- [[product-management-in-the-ai-era]] — Singhal's builder-vs-information-mover split: AI eats the information-moving half of the PM job while judgment becomes the bottleneck (10–100× more changes), the shed-30k/rehire-8k-AI-first prediction against a record-high open-PM-roles market, brand depreciating relative to how modern you are, the shadow superpower and equal-disappointment algorithm as the reasons strong performers don't reinvent, joy-and-obsolescence as the crossing mechanism, PMs dispersing outward (CHRO, founder) while designers/engineers flow in, the design plateau as production-vs-taste, and the hardware→internet PM precedent that says the chaos settles in ~2 years
